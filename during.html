During Your Experience

Manage active project work, communicate with partners, gather feedback, and adapt responsibly when circumstances change.

Navigate Your Active Project

The middle of an engaged learning project is where real growth happens and where real challenges surface. Requirements shift, team dynamics evolve, partners face unexpected constraints, and research may produce surprising results.

Developing resilience, adaptability, and ethical judgment is part of the work. When you treat friction as information instead of failure, your team can revisit assumptions, communicate openly, and make better decisions with the people affected by the project.

Active-Project Objectives

  • Fieldwork and research methods: Use human-centered tools to observe context, listen to participants, and turn evidence into insights.
  • Sponsor and client updates: Maintain transparency through regular, concise progress reporting.
  • Feedback loops: Gather, evaluate, and integrate constructive feedback throughout the project instead of waiting until the end.
  • Project health: Monitor scope, risks, team capacity, deadlines, and the continuing value of the work.

Use a Weekly Project Rhythm

  1. Review the purpose. Connect this week's tasks to the partner's goal and the intended users.
  2. Check the plan. Review milestones, owners, dependencies, risks, and decisions that are waiting for input.
  3. Do the work. Complete research, analysis, design, development, testing, or documentation in manageable increments.
  4. Share progress. Communicate what changed, what evidence supports the change, and what the partner needs to review.
  5. Invite feedback. Ask focused questions early enough that feedback can affect the next iteration.
  6. Reflect and adjust. Record decisions, update the scope or timeline when needed, and identify the next most valuable step.

Respond to Common Project Challenges

Questions and actions for common challenges during an engaged learning project
Challenge Ask Try
Scope is growing Which request best supports the agreed project goal, and what would have to change to include it? Document the proposed change, estimate its effect on time and quality, and seek shared approval before committing.
Feedback conflicts Whose needs, evidence, and decision-making authority should guide this choice? Summarize the options and tradeoffs, then confirm the decision and reasoning in writing.
A teammate is blocked Is the barrier related to information, skill, time, access, role clarity, or team dynamics? Redistribute work, pair teammates, request support, or revise the plan before the delay becomes a crisis.
Partner communication slows Can the team make a reversible decision, or is partner input essential? Send a concise status update with a clear question, deadline, and safe default while documenting assumptions.
Research raises an ethical concern Could the method or result harm, exclude, expose, or misrepresent anyone? Pause the affected work and consult the instructor, project lead, or appropriate research oversight resource.

Pause for an Ethical Check

Return to these questions whenever the team changes a research method, audience, tool, data source, or deliverable:

  • Do participants understand how their information or contributions will be used?
  • Are we collecting only the information the project genuinely needs?
  • Whose perspective is represented, and whose perspective may be missing?
  • Could our process or deliverable create barriers for people with disabilities?
  • Are we protecting confidential, private, or sensitive information?
  • Have we clearly separated evidence from assumptions?
  • Can the partner maintain and use what we are creating?

Reflect While You Work

Short, regular reflection helps the team recognize learning before details are forgotten. Consider discussing one question at the end of each team meeting:

  • What did we learn this week that changed our understanding of the project?
  • What decision are we most confident about, and what evidence supports it?
  • Where are we still making assumptions?
  • How did we use partner or user feedback?
  • What should we stop, start, or continue next week?

Ask for Support Early

Bring emerging risks, relationship concerns, accessibility questions, or ethical uncertainty to your instructor or program lead before they grow. For general ELO support, email umsi.engagement@umich.edu.
