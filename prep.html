Get ready before you commit

You bring useful skills, but the partner brings knowledge of their own community. Begin with questions rather than a proposed solution.

  1. Understand the context. Read about the organization, the people it serves, and any earlier work on the project.
  2. Check your assumptions. Discuss how your identity, experiences, and privileges might shape what you notice or overlook.
  3. Set learning goals. Name the technical, professional, and interpersonal skills you want to develop.
  4. Review team capacity. Compare skills and availability with program expectations before agreeing to deliverables.

Bring these questions to the team

  • Who will use or be affected by this work?
  • Whose knowledge or perspective is missing from our team?
  • How will the partner take part in decisions?
  • What would a useful, realistic outcome look like for everyone?

Preparation resources

Choose a tool for the task in front of you. Some documents may require your U-M account.

  • Social Identity Snapshot - Reflect on self-awareness and positionality before entering community contexts.
  • “Check Yourself” Community Engagement Checklist - Audit personal assumptions and ethical readiness.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation - Understand levels of community involvement and decision-making power.
  • Project Scoping Handout and Terms - Translate partner needs into realistic project goals.
  • Student Organization Officer Guide - Review roles and commitments for student organization leaders.
  • Project Manager Guide - Clarify expectations for student project leads.
  • Project Manager Commitment Form - Document responsibilities for student leads.
  • Project Management Worksheet - Clarify objectives, roles, timelines, and what can be shared with the client.
